What we store, and what we will never do with it.

Stuckato is used by children. This page is written to be read by a parent in five minutes, not by a lawyer. If anything here is unclear, that is our fault and we want to know.

Last updated 23 November 2026.

The short version

  • We store what you put in, and nothing else. There is no tracking, no advertising and no analytics of any kind on this site.
  • Only your teacher and you can see your practice. No other pupil, no other teacher, no one outside your studio.
  • We do not sell anything to anyone, ever. There is no version of Stuckato where your child's data is the product.
  • You can delete everything yourself, from inside the app, at any time. It is immediate and it is permanent.

Exactly what is stored

WhatWhy it exists

A display nameSo your teacher knows whose week is whose. A first name is enough.
Instrument and levelSo the week is written for the right instrument.

Two weekly ratingsHow ready you feel for the lesson, and how much you wanted to practise. Each is a number from 1 (not at all) to 5 (completely).
Audio recordingsOnly when someone chooses to record: the teacher's passage, or a pupil's play-through. Stored in the studio's folder.
Backing tracksOnly if the teacher switches them on and the pupil keeps a track: the pupil's recording plus the chords and style chosen, in a private folder only the pupil, the account holder and their teacher can open. Up to five a week. The backing itself is made on the device; no audio is sent anywhere to make it. A teacher's exam accompaniment is stored privately too, for their studio only. If the teacher links a backing track on YouTube instead, it plays in YouTube's privacy-enhanced player (youtube-nocookie.com), so YouTube receives what any embedded video sends; nothing about the pupil is passed to it, and nothing is copied from YouTube. The pupil's recording with it (audio, or video for adults who choose it) is stored the same private way.
Free playOnly if the teacher switches it on: on which day the pupil played over a backing loop, for how long, the key and style the teacher chose, and the pupil's answer to "Did you play anything you liked?". Nothing is recorded and the microphone is not used. The loop is made on the device.
Teacher voice notesA teacher can record up to a minute after a lesson for one pupil. It is stored privately in the studio's folder and only that pupil, the account holder and the teacher can play it.
Listen backA pupil can record two short takes and play them back to compare. They stay on the phone or tablet and are thrown away when the pupil leaves the screen: nothing is uploaded or kept, except which take the pupil said sounded better.
Sight-readingWhich short piece the pupil was given and how they rated it. A recording of their try only if the teacher switches recording on (and, for a pupil under 13, a grown-up confirms); it is stored privately like other recordings, for the pupil, the account holder and the teacher, and the teacher can send one line back.
Weekly summary emailOn the morning of each lesson, the account holder (a parent, for a pupil under 13) gets one email with the week: days and minutes practised, what was worked on, the teacher's last line and the quiz result. It can be switched off in the app under Goal.
Games and the ear warm-upScores from the warm-up games (stars, how many echoes matched), how the pupil rated a review item, the speed reached on the tempo ladder, which weekly quests are done, which treat card was picked, a guess at the week's mystery tune and lesson-day quiz scores, all kept with the day's practice. In "Echo it" the pupil can sing or play three or four notes back: the microphone is listened to on the device to compare the notes, and the sound is then thrown away. Only if the teacher switches it on, for a pupil aged 13 or over whose family agreed to sound recordings, is a take the app could not hear clearly saved for the teacher to listen to, the same private way as other recordings. Nothing in the games marks a note wrong.
A cloned teaching voiceOnly if a teacher opts in, and only of the teacher. Never of a pupil. Removable from the app.

That is the complete list. If you want to see your own copy of it, ask your teacher or email us and we will send it.

Who can see it

Your teacher, for their own pupils only. This is enforced by the database itself, not by a promise in the app: every row carries a rule saying it can only be read by the pupil it belongs to or the teacher of their studio.

Us, only when we have to. The person who maintains the database can reach it. We look at an individual account only to fix a fault someone has reported, and we do not read pupils' sentences for interest.

Nobody else. No other pupil, no other teacher, no school, no advertiser, no data broker.

What we will never do

  • Never sell or share your data, or let anyone else use it for their own purposes.
  • Never show advertising, and never build a profile for advertising.
  • Never run third-party trackers or analytics. There is no Google Analytics, no Meta pixel, nothing of that kind, and there never will be while children use this.
  • Never clone a child's voice. Voice cloning is offered to teachers, for their own voice, and only if they ask for it.
  • Never use your practice to train a general AI model.

Where it lives, and who else touches it

Stuckato is a small operation, so it is built on services other people run. Each one only sees the part it needs to do its job.

ServiceWhat it handles
SupabaseThe database, sign-in and audio storage. This is where your practice data actually sits.
VercelServes the website and runs the small functions behind it.
ResendSends the sign-in link and reminder emails. It sees your email address.
AnthropicTurns the teacher's note into the week's sessions. It sees the note and the pupil's first name, not your email or your history. When a teacher asks, it also drafts fact cards about a piece: for that it sees only the piece's title, its composer and the instrument, nothing about the pupil, and the teacher approves every card before a pupil sees it. For Collaborate it sees the teacher's general description of a group (how many players, instruments, levels, an age range, what to focus on), never names.
ElevenLabsOnly for teachers: if a teacher opts into a cloned voice, it sees that teacher's recordings; if a teacher dictates a lesson note in a browser without built-in dictation, it turns that recording into text. It never receives anything from a pupil.

The backing-track instruments are recordings that come with the app and play on your device: the Salamander Grand Piano by Alexander Holm, and cello, violin and guitar from tonejs-instruments by Nicholaus Brosowsky, both under CC BY 3.0. No service is involved in making a backing.

How long we keep it

While the account exists, so that a pupil can look back at earlier weeks and a teacher can see progress over a term. When an account is deleted, everything attached to it goes immediately, including the recordings.

Backups are the one exception, and we would rather say so than pretend otherwise: deleted data can sit in Supabase's routine backups for up to thirty days before it ages out. Nobody reads those backups, and they are not used to restore individual accounts.

Deleting everything

From inside the app. Sign in, and use Delete my account. It removes your account, your practice data and your recordings straight away. It cannot be undone.

A teacher with pupils cannot delete in one tap, on purpose. Deleting a studio would delete every pupil in it, so email us and we will do it carefully with you.

By asking. Email hello@stuckato.app and we will do it for you. A parent can ask for their child's data to be deleted at any time and does not need a reason.

Children

Stuckato is designed for children learning an instrument, and for a young child the account is held by a parent. The child still does the practising and the ticking; the account, and every decision about the data, belongs to the adult.

We ask for as little as we can get away with. There is no profile photo, no date of birth, no address, no phone number, and no way for one child to contact another. There is no public profile and nothing a pupil does is visible outside their own studio.

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask to see your data, correct it, have it deleted, or object to what we do with it. In practice, all four are one email to hello@stuckato.app, and we will answer inside a month. If you are unhappy with our answer you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
